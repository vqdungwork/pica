## Purpose

The plan exists so that what comes back can be compared, counted and sourced. Without it you
get five conversations and no findings.

## Input

**Works best with:** the scope boundary and the field.
**Also useful:** who the client can actually introduce you to, and by when.

Anything already supplied — a segment list, an existing persona deck — counts as given. Use it
and say which parts you are treating as unverified.

## The method

**1. State what would change your mind.** A study that cannot produce a surprising answer is
a study that will not produce one. Write the finding that would make you recommend against
building this.

**2. Define segments by job and context of use, never demographics.** *"Pharmacist assembling
during the 4pm rush"* is a segment. *"35–50, urban"* is a marketing slide.

**3. Write the screener before the guide.** It is the thing that stops you interviewing five
people the client likes. At least one participant must be someone who **said no** to the
current solution.

**4. Write questions about the last time, not about usually.** *"Walk me through the last
prescription you couldn't find"* beats *"how often do you lose prescriptions"* — the second
collects an estimate, the first collects an event.

**5. Fix the return schema before fanning out.** Every researcher returns the same fields, or
the findings cannot be merged. A pain point needs a frequency, an evidence class and a source.

## What good looks like

Two researchers running the same guide on different segments produce findings you can put in
one table without translating.

## Common failures

**Only talking to users the client chose.** They are the ones who stayed.

**Asking people to predict.** *"Would you use this?"* measures politeness.

**No evidence class.** Observed, reported and inferred are three different confidences, and a
table that does not distinguish them is one confidence: unknown.

## Done when

The plan names its segments with a job and a context, the screener includes a refuser, and the
return schema is written down before anybody is spawned.
