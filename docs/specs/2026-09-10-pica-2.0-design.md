# pica 2.0 — design-only, contract-first

**Status:** approved design, not yet implemented
**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Branch:** `pica-2.0-design-only`
**Release:** a single 2.0.0 after all ten stages are green locally. No intermediate release.

---

## 1 · What pica becomes

A **discovery and design engagement** that ends at three things the client confirms together:

1. a **PRD** they agree is correct
2. a **scope of work** they agree to
3. a **working interactive demo** that behaves as they expect

The effort estimate is produced by the operator, outside pica. pica no longer writes code, tests,
architecture, or prices.

## 2 · Three knowledge axes

| File | Shape | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `industries.json` *(exists)* | 28 sectors + `ambiguous` | colour, style, density, typography, tone, forbidden, stakeholders, standards, regulator, requiredConstraints |
| **`audiences.json`** *(new)* | **dimensions with values**, composable | age band · region/culture · accessibility · digital literacy · conditions of use |
| **`archetypes.json`** *(new)* | **per application** | core screens · navigation shape · primary object · density · anti-patterns · reference products |

**Conflict rule.** Audience **accessibility floors override** sector density and stylistic preference.
Sector retains colour meaning, tone and forbidden patterns. Floors are floors, not preferences.

**Embedding.** A generator writes each agent's embedded copy of all three axes from the single source.
`knowledge-drift-check` re-derives and fails when an embedded copy no longer matches. Agents are
self-contained at runtime; maintenance stays single-source.

**Refusal.** An unresolvable sector is refused, as today. An unstated audience is **researched**, not
refused — see §4.

## 3 · Phase 1 · intake — 5 inputs

| # | Input | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brief** | a **list** of documents, any format (text, `.docx`, `.pdf`, `.pptx` — new capability). Verbatim, source language, **read-only forever**. Absence declared in `briefAbsent`. Changes go in append-only `docs/brief-amendments.md`, read by `brief-cold` alongside the original. |
| 2 | **Sources** | **graded authority**: `authoritative` (tokens may be `taken`) · `reference` (measured, never taken) · `historical` (context only) · `ignore`. An inaccessible `use` source **blocks** and names itself. |
| 3 | **Environment** | which MCP servers and tools are live · what only the human can do. pica self-verifies `playwright`, node, package manager, a free port. |
| 4 | **Field** | the sector, named narrowly. Resolves against `industries.json`. |
| 5 | **Archetype** | **per application**, against `archetypes.json`. |

Inputs 4 and 5 are **derived from the brief** where the brief states them, marked `derived` with the
section they came from, then **confirmed once** before research runs — showing the value, its source,
and the two or three constraints that follow.

### Removed from intake, and where each went

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| Commercial constraint | dropped. Only the disclosure policy mattered; it moves to the engagement scope at stop ① and is enforced by `disclosure-check` |
| Figma declaration | dropped. Asked at the port decision, after the freeze; the MCP is checked then too |
| Fonts installed | dropped. Design is font-free; family names live in tokens and on the kit page; `font-check` verifies at handoff |
| Trigger | removed. Discovery researches *why buyers change* instead |
| Audience | moved to discover |
| 1d delivery options | cut |

### Outputs

`docs/brief.md` (or the `briefPath` list) · the **1b limitations statement** before any capability
claim · `docs/contract.md` — the **engagement** contract (work packages with acceptance criteria in
the human's own terms, viewports each with an **idiom**, applications each with its **archetype**,
banned characters) · `docs/exclusions.md` quoted then asked then `exclusionsConfirmed` · package
tiering · scaffold and `.pica/state.json`.

**⏸ Stop ① — engagement signed.** Scope, exclusions, disclosure policy.

**Moved out:** the data-ownership table leaves intake. It is a CRUD matrix and belongs in analyse,
after the entities exist.

## 4 · Phase 2 · discover

| | |
|---|---|
| Requires | `state.field` · `state.archetype` |
| Produces | `state.discovery` · `state.stakeholders` · **`state.audience`** · **`state.operatingModel`** · **AS-IS process model** · `docs/research/{pain-points,stakeholders,competitors,market,operating-model}.md` |
| Grants | `researchComplete` |
| Check | `discover-check` (extended) · **`audience-check`** |

**Kept unchanged:** three lists never merged (Users / Buyers / Stakeholders) · evidence class checked
against confidence, `inferred` capped at medium · frequency as `nOf` not adjectives · five interviews
per segment with `shortfallWhy` · somebody who said no, or `saidNoWhyNone` · context of use · market
derived and multiplied · competitor **pricing and packaging** · switching cost · ranked by frequency
and severity.

**Added:**

- **Audience profile**, proportional to what the brief already says. Brief names the target user →
  a short confirmation. Brief silent → full audience research. Never assumed.
- **Client operating model** — how the business earns and who does what operationally. This is what
  the admin console has to mirror.
- **AS-IS process model** — moved here from analyse because pica's own rule demands *"observation over
  description: the live product, the analytics, the support logs."* Produced here, it inherits
  discovery's `observed` / `stated` / `inferred` classes, so a step somebody described but nobody
  watched is visibly marked.

**⏸ Confirmation — the audience profile** with its evidence and the floors it sets, after discovery and
**before tokens exist**, so the floors shape the design system rather than being retrofitted.

## 5 · Phase 3 · research

| | |
|---|---|
| Requires | `docs/contract.md` · gate `intakeApproved` · **`state.audience`** |
| Produces | `tokens/tokens.json` · `tokens/tokens.css` · **`state.measured`** · **internal moodboard** |
| Grants | `tokensApproved` |
| Check | `schema-check` |

**Changed:**

- The **intake rules move out** to `packages/core/rules/intake.md`. Today `pica.md` requires loading
  the research package's `research.md` before anything else, so intake cannot run without research
  installed — which defeats entering at any phase.
- Research now **requires the audience profile**, so the type scale and colour ramp are derived inside
  the floors from the start.
- `state.measured` is **declared** — today it is produced by nobody while picaflow references it.

**Internal moodboard.** The researcher retains visual reference — captures of the measured products,
extracted palettes with hex values, style signatures, case studies from adjacent fields — each with
provenance (which product, which URL, captured when). **Never client-facing.** It does not appear in
`review.html`, the demo, or handover.

**New rule: never show the client a reference you cannot match.** Naming a product with numbers is an
argument that survives a review. Showing a beautiful screenshot beside your own build invites a
comparison you will lose, and that is what produces disappointment at handover.

## 6 · Phase 4 · analyse — two agents, three stages

| Stage | Agent | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | `pica-analyst` | problem · glossary + **data dictionary** · business rules `BR-nn` · domain constraints (8 categories) · **NFRs as numbers** |
| **B** | **`pica-modeller`** *(new)* | **TO-BE process model** · **domain model** · **roles & permissions CRUD matrix** · **state model** |
| **C** | `pica-analyst` | use cases `UC-nn` · **requirements classified** · **journey maps** · delta · **PRD** |

The interleave is deliberate: the domain model needs the glossary, and the use cases need the entities.

### Requirements classification — BABOK

Every requirement carries a class: **business** → **stakeholder** → **solution**, and solution splits
into **functional**, **non-functional**, and **transition**. Transition requirements exist only to get
from the current state to the future one and are flagged temporary, so bulk import and manual entry
are not designed as permanent features.

### Process modelling — notation is now required

Today AS-IS and TO-BE are four sentences of prose with no notation, and `trace-check` only asserts that
a non-empty string exists. The words BPMN and swimlane appear nowhere in the repository.

**Rule:** **BPMN with lanes when the process crosses roles; a flowchart when it does not.** Choosing
wrong costs either way — a BPMN model for a three-step approval confuses stakeholders, a flowchart for
a multi-role process hides the handoffs.

Rendered archify-style: typed JSON (`lanes`, `nodes`, `edges`) → schema validation → self-contained
SVG. The same mechanism renders pica's own flow diagram.

### Journey maps — one per actor with a lane

BPMN answers *who does what and where it branches*. A journey map answers *where it hurts*. They are
one model with two views: **a journey map is that actor's BPMN lane unrolled in time with pain and
opportunity layered on.**

- One journey **per actor that has a lane in the process model**. A veto holder who never touches the
  product gets none.
- The **stage axis is derived from that actor's lane**, never hand-written, so the two views cannot
  disagree about sequence.
- Pain carries `nOf` and evidence class **traced back to discovery**; nothing new is invented here.

### Roles & permissions matrix

BABOK 10.39, as a **CRUD matrix** — roles × entities → Create / Read / Update / Delete. pica already
demanded a data-ownership table, but at intake, before the entities were known. It moves here.

### Checks

Existing: `trace-check` · `domain-check` · `industry-check` · `problem-check` *(minus the now-dead
`trigger` and `commercialConstraint` assertions)*

New: **`process-check`** (every activity in a lane · every gateway ≥2 outgoing · no dead ends · every
activity traces to a use case) · **`permissions-check`** (every entity × role has a value · no
unowned entity · no permission a use case does not grant) · **`nfr-check`** (a number with a unit,
never an adjective) · **`requirements-class-check`** · **`journey-check`** (every laned actor has a
journey · stages match that lane · pain traces to discovery)

## 7 · Phase 5 · design — 9 steps, 3 gates

| Step | Who | Produces | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D0** IA, screen inventory traced to use cases, data source per screen | `pica-designer` | `state.screens` | |
| **D1** lo-fi in HTML, greyscale, real content lengths | `pica-designer` | `html/structure/` | **▸ structure** |
| **D2** three directions — same busiest screen three ways | main thread, **never an agent** | `state.proposals` | **▸ direction** |
| **D3** foundations page | `pica-designer` | `tokens/`, `design-system.html` | **⏸ design system** |
| **D4** every screen, every viewport, every state | `pica-designer` | `html/` | |
| **D5** the words, bound to the glossary | `pica-writer` | `state.copyRules` | |
| **D6** measure — capture, then the measured checks | scripts | `.audit/html-reference*.json` | |
| **D7** evaluate ×3–5, one lens each, no write access | `pica-evaluator` | `docs/reviews/` | |
| **D8** **`demo.html`** — React, responsive, interactive, mock data, motion behaviour, served | `pica-designer` | the demo, captured via `--url` | |
| **D9** *optional* port, verified frame by frame | main thread | `.audit/figma-dump.json` | |

**The state matrix has left D0** — it is now `state.stateModel`, produced by the modeller in analyse.
The designer receives it rather than inventing it. This is what makes state coverage checkable for the
first time.

**D3 contains:** palette with roles and **computed** contrast · type scale at real sizes · spacing scale ·
kit with every component in every state · **icon set** (name, licence, stroke weight, grid) · **motion
tokens** (duration scale, easing, `prefers-reduced-motion`).

Icon set and motion tokens get **no separate step**. Neither is a choice — both follow from the chosen
direction — and pica's rule is that a slot with one option is a decision being shown, not a proposal.
D3 is presented **for approval, not for re-choosing**; if it is wrong, the answer is a second D2 round.

**Three directions stay three**, with a new requirement that they be **measurably far apart** — three
different traditions, differing in density, type scale and elevation handling. If none lands, run a
second round of three informed by what was rejected. That is cheaper and better than six upfront, and
it protects the rule that you never offer an option you would refuse to build.

**Boards stay static HTML; the flow becomes React.** The rule changes from *"no build step to view a
screen"* to *"no build step to view a **board**."* Boards are decision records and are never ported.

**Evaluation is agents only.** No real-user requirement. The closeout report must therefore continue to
state plainly that no human has used it — see §12.

### Checks

Existing: `verify-html` · `parity-check` · `contrast-check` · `spacing-check` · `coverage-check` ·
`flow-check` *(gains `--url`)* · `shell-check` *(rewritten for the React shell)* · `concept-check` ·
`code-tokens-check` · `copy-check` · `geometry-diff` · `build-diff` *(moved from `build`)*

New: **`structure-check`** · **`direction-spread-check`** · **`foundations-check`**

`foundations-check` is written to pica's own specification, which the rules state and nothing
implements: *"a check that every token in `tokens/tokens.json` appears on the page, and that every
class a screen uses appears there with at least one variant."* Plus: every colour pair meets the
audience floor · every component has every state · every token carries provenance · the icon set is
named and licensed · every icon used exists in that set · no emoji stands in for an icon · icon stroke
weight matches the direction's declared value.

## 10 · The chain that prevents a missing screen

Four links, four distinct failure modes, no gap between them.

| # | Link | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `state.stateModel` *(modeller, analyse)* | — the first machine-readable list of states pica has had |
| 2 | **`state-coverage-check`** *(new)* | a state that was never built |
| 3 | **`frame-inventory-check`** *(new)* | a frame missing from — or extra in — the Figma port |
| 4 | `geometry-diff` *(exists)* | a present frame in the wrong position |

**Why link 2 is needed.** `coverage-check` verifies use case × viewport and says so explicitly. Nothing
reads the state matrix — the rule names eight minimum states (default, loading, empty, error, disabled,
keyboard-open, focus) and calls writing it *"the cheapest possible way to avoid finding a missing state
during handoff"*, but no check enforces it. A happy-path screen at every viewport passes green today.

`state-coverage-check` compares `screens × viewports × states` against what the capture contains.
States are **tagged** (`data-state="empty"`), never inferred, for the same reason `data-viewport` and
`data-uc` are. "Not applicable" must be **declared with a reason** — an unasked question and a null
answer look identical otherwise.

**Why link 3 is needed.** `geometry-diff` documents its own blindness: *"WHAT THIS CANNOT DO: detect
absence… Frame inventory and text-run counts are `parity-check.mjs`'s and `verify-html.mjs`'s job."*
But `parity-check` compares **viewport to viewport within the HTML** — it never receives the Figma dump
— and states outright *"WHAT IT DOES NOT CHECK: the set of screen names present at each viewport."*
`verify-html` is also HTML-only. **A check delegates to a check that does not accept the job**, so a
port that drops five frames passes everything.

`frame-inventory-check` compares the set of `(pkg, frame, viewport)` in the reference against the Figma
dump, failing on frames missing from Figma *and* frames in Figma that were never designed. It pairs via
the declared frame map, per `reference-discipline.md`, never by name.

**Composition.** Link 2 makes link 3 stronger for free: once the reference contains every state,
verifying Figma has every reference frame also verifies it has every state.

### Two more silent-wrong-number guards

The React demo introduces measurement risks static files do not have. Both are the same class as a
webfont silently falling back — no error, correct-looking page, completely wrong numbers.

- **`capture-settled`** — before measuring, assert the route is stable: no pending requests, no layout
  shift within N ms. Not settled → fail, never measure anyway.
- **`font-check`** — record which family actually painted each text run and fail when it is a fallback
  rather than the declared face. Runs as a **handoff gate**, not a design gate.

## 12 · Deliberately not included

**Real-user testing.** Evaluation is 3–5 `pica-evaluator` agents with distinct lenses plus a cognitive
walkthrough per use case — an established method that recognises up to 80% of usability problems, and
one that exists precisely because users are not always available. It is **not** a substitute for real
users: it cannot find what a practitioner spots in one second, and it cannot tell you whether anyone
wants the thing.

pica therefore continues to require its closing report to state, explicitly, that **no human has looked
at this yet.** That line is the honest cost of this decision and must not be softened.

## 13 · Work plan — 10 stages, one release

Branch off `main`. Every stage green before the next. No tag, no release, no marketplace bump until
stage 10 passes.

| # | Stage | Green when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contracts made honest · `contract-check` · gate reconciliation | fails on today's manifests, passes after |
| 2 | Delete 6 packages · strip dead assertions · re-author `examples/approvals` | full CI green on 8 packages |
| 3 | `audiences.json` · `archetypes.json` · embedding generator · `knowledge-drift-check` | regenerate produces an empty diff |
| 4 | Intake to 5 inputs · rules move to `core` · discover gains audience, operating model, AS-IS · research requires audience | derived chain order matches intended order |
| 5 | Split `pica-modeller` · BABOK classification · process model · journey maps · 5 new checks | each new check fails on a planted defect |
| 6 | Structure gate · foundations gate · direction spread | each exits non-zero when unmet |
| 7 | React `demo.html` · `flow-check --url` · capture `--url` · `capture-settled` · `state-coverage-check` | a planted routing defect and a planted missing state are both caught |
| 8 | Font de-constraint · `font-check` at handoff · substitutions reported at port | a design using an uninstalled font builds and ports with a reported substitution |
| 9 | `frame-inventory-check` · place or delete the two unplaced figma checks | a port with a dropped frame fails |
| 10 | README · banner · marketplace · **recount** · flow diagram generated from the manifest graph | `count-test` green |

**Stage 1 is load-bearing.** It makes the front/back boundary machine-checkable, which is what lets
stage 4 replace the front half freely and stage 2 delete packages without hand-editing a chain.

**Entering at any phase** — the requirement that someone arriving with a PRD can use design alone —
depends on stages 1 and 4. A phase runs when its declared inputs exist, regardless of whether pica
produced them. `pica-verify --adopt` already lists what is missing; a new `/pica-adopt` maps existing
client documents into state with provenance, so close can still prove against the original brief.
